# Villagers Plus

Adds four new villager professions — **Alchemist**, **Occultist**, **Horticulturist**, and **Oceanographer** — each with their own workstation building and trade pool, and each buildable into any of CTOV/Towns and Towers' biome village variants (plains, taiga, savanna, snowy, desert weights are all configured per-biome in `config/villagersplus.json5`; the Oceanographer's workstation is weighted highest at 15 versus 10 for the others in every biome). Villagers gain up to 2 new trade offers per profession level (max), and the Wandering Trader can carry up to 5.

The standout mechanical detail: the **Alchemist's workstation can explode**. `can_explode` is enabled with an `explosion_chance` of "1 in 3" per interaction — a genuine risk/reward element baked into what's otherwise a peaceful villager-trading mod. There's also an **Enchanted Basin** that stores up to 500 XP, transferring 50 per interaction, letting players bank experience with a villager rather than only via bottles.

This is the fourth of the pack's village-overhaul mods, but unlike the other three it doesn't touch village generation at all — it only adds new professions/trades/workstations that populate whatever villages CTOV, Towns and Towers, and Integrated Villages produce.

## Anticipated questions

**Q: What new villagers does it add?**
A: Four professions — Alchemist, Occultist, Horticulturist, and Oceanographer — each with its own workstation and trade pool, buildable into CTOV / Towns and Towers village variants.

**Q: Is there any risk to trading with them?**
A: Yes — the Alchemist's workstation can explode, with `can_explode` enabled at a 1-in-3 chance per interaction: a genuine risk/reward element.

**Q: Can I store XP with a villager?**
A: Yes — the Enchanted Basin stores up to 500 XP, transferring 50 per interaction.

**Q: Does it change village generation?**
A: No — unlike the other three village mods, it only adds professions/trades/workstations to whatever villages CTOV, Towns and Towers, and Integrated Villages produce.
